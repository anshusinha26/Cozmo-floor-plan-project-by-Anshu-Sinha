# Cozmo technical report

Handheld phone capture to a dimensioned floor plan with damage annotations.

Every number below comes from a file in this repository and names the script
that rebuilds it. Anything not yet measured is marked PENDING with what it
waits on. `scripts/regenerate_all.sh` rebuilds all of it.

---

## 1. Architecture

One contract, one backend, three front ends.

**The contract first.** `cozmo/contracts/models.py` defines the plan. Its rule
is that no dimension is ever a bare number: every `*_m`, `*_m2` and `*_deg`
field is a `Measurement` carrying `value`, `ci_low`, `ci_high`, `unit`,
`method` and `ci_level`. A test walks emitted JSON and fails on any bare
number (`tests/test_contract.py`). Referential integrity is checked at load
time, so a plan that parses is a plan whose graph is consistent, and the
evaluator never repairs input before scoring it. The schema is generated from
the models by `cozmo schema`, so code and schema cannot drift.

**The shared backend.** Tiers differ only in how they produce geometry. Once
a tier has a point cloud with poses, everything downstream is shared:
`cozmo/lidar/levels.py` (floor and ceiling), `walls.py` (Manhattan frame and
wall faces), `rooms.py` and `cells.py` (rooms), `openings.py` (doors and
adjacency), `uncertainty.py` (intervals), `render/plan.py` (drawing). Damage
is separate and tier-agnostic: `cozmo/damage/` takes an iterator of frames,
each an image plus optionally depth, intrinsics and pose, and the plan's
surfaces.

**Provenance.** `run` writes `plan.json`, `plan.png`, `run_manifest.json`,
`drift_report.json` and `debug/`. The manifest holds a SHA-256 of every input
file the tier is allowed to read, the resolved config and its hash, the git
commit, the seed, per-stage timings and library versions. The plan itself
holds no wall-clock field, so the same input, config and seed produce a
byte-identical `plan.json`; a test asserts it.

**Tier isolation is enforced in code, not by convention.** Every file access
goes through `StrayScan.open`, which raises on a file the tier may not read:
the video tier sees only `rgb.mp4`, the photo tier is refused a scan folder
outright because `depth/` holds png files that would otherwise pass as room
photos. The run manifest hashes only the permitted files, so provenance never
touches data the tier could not have used (`tests/test_stray.py`).

## 2. Tiers and devices

| tier | input | reconstruction | state |
|---|---|---|---|
| lidar | one Stray Scanner scan for the whole property | classical geometry, no ML | done |
| video | one clip per room | SfM plus monocular depth, separate branch | PENDING, waits on loop 2 after-run |
| photo | 2 to 8 stills per room | SfM plus monocular depth, separate branch | PENDING, waits on the photo tier |

**Stray Scanner format.** `rgb.mp4` is 1920x1440 HEVC at 60 fps, stored
rotated for a portrait capture; `depth/NNNNNN.png` is 256x192 uint16
millimetres; `confidence/` is 0, 1, 2 and only 2 is used; `odometry.csv` gives
camera-to-world poses with quaternions in x, y, z, w order and per-frame
intrinsics for the full frame, scaled by 256/1920 for depth. Unprojection is
OpenCV convention, `X = (u-cx)/fx*d`, then `world = R p + t`. Getting this
wrong is silent, so it is pinned by a regression test: on `c00a170fe1` the
floor must appear as a peak at world y = -1.48 m within 2 cm
(`tests/test_stray.py::test_real_scan_floor_peak_near_minus_1_48`).

**Devices.** Nokia 8.1 shot every video loop and the `bedroom_2_repeat`
stills; Moto Edge 50 Neo shot the hall, bedroom_1, bedroom_2 and kitchen
stills. EXIF make and model were stripped from the supplied files, so device
attribution is the operator's record and the ground-truth files say so. Read
from the files themselves: stills are 1200x1600, clips are 3840x2160 H.264 at
about 30 fps. **No iPhone was available for capture**: every LiDAR result comes
from the three supplied scans, so the LiDAR capture route in
`docs/capture_protocol.md` is written from the format and has not been walked
through on our own device. Full table in `docs/device_matrix.md`.

**Runtime**, `fix_loop/*/bench/runs/*/run_manifest.json`, regenerated by
`fix_loop/regenerate.sh`: 4.0 s for 1715 frames, 13.2 s for 5251, 27.8 s for
9745, on an Apple M1 Max. The budget was 3 minutes for the largest scan.

### Video tier: what was rejected and why

**MapAnything was tested and rejected** (`docs/experiments/mapanything/result.json`).
Image-only inference on `c00a170fe1` at 20, 40 and 80 frames ran on MPS with
no operator fallback, 21 s to 141 s. Its metric scale is wrong by about 30%,
consistently: the median ratio of predicted depth to LiDAR depth over
confidence-2 pixels is 0.712, 0.704 and 0.709 across the three runs, standard
deviation 0.09 to 0.13. A fixed correction would leave roughly 13% residual
spread, so this is a systematic scale error with real per-frame noise on top,
not a calibration. The fused cloud does not produce straight walls; the
top-down density is a smear extending 11 m from a room a few metres across
(`docs/experiments/mapanything/topdown_density_n40.png`).

Feeding it the ARKit poses and intrinsics would likely have fixed the scale,
and it was refused: the video tier has no poses and no intrinsics. A video
result obtained by handing the model LiDAR-derived poses measures the LiDAR
tier, not the video tier, and would not survive the walk-in test on a phone
without a depth sensor.

**The chosen route is SfM plus Depth Pro**, with the monocular depth scaled by
the focal length COLMAP recovers rather than by a learned scale prior. That
keeps scale traceable to the reconstruction rather than to a network. Accuracy
PENDING, waits on the loop 2 after-run on the video branch.

## 3. Drift handling

ARKit odometry drifts slowly: yaw creeps and floor height wanders over a few
minutes of walking, which smears wall faces and breaks the floor into a wedge.

`cozmo/lidar/drift.py` anchors to the building rather than to the trajectory.
The walk is cut into 5-second chunks. Per chunk: the dominant wall azimuth
modulo 90 degrees gives a yaw error against the global Manhattan direction,
removed by rotating about the chunk's own centroid so it does not swing across
the map; the median height of its floor points against the global floor plane
gives a vertical offset; then the chunk's wall points are shifted along each
horizontal axis by the median offset to the nearest global wall face.
Corrections beyond the configured limits are rejected rather than applied,
because a chunk that looks 20 degrees off usually has too little wall to
estimate from.

**The ablation says it barely matters on this data.** From
`fix_loop/after/bench/runs/*/drift_report.json`, rebuilt by
`fix_loop/regenerate.sh`:

| scan | wall thickness off | on | footprint off | on |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| c00a170fe1 | 24.2 mm | 23.6 mm | 55.4 m2 | 35.8 m2 |
| 1a8384c3f6 | 32.1 mm | 35.8 mm | 80.0 m2 | 86.6 m2 |
| c7d28f72c6 | 32.5 mm | 32.6 mm | 65.8 m2 | 64.7 m2 |

Wall thickness improves on one scan, worsens on another, and is unchanged on
the third. The reason is in the model summary: mean yaw error 0.55 to 1.57
degrees, mean height error 3 to 8 mm, mean shift 1.4 to 2.2 cm. **ARKit's
poses were already good enough that there was little to correct.** Room
overlap does fall consistently (0.39 to 0.27 m2 on the largest scan). The
correction is on by default because it costs one extra pass and does no harm,
and that is the whole case for it.

## 4. Error budget

Every interval is built from named terms, defined in
`cozmo/lidar/uncertainty.py`.

**Wall length.** Two independent parts. Statistical: each bounding wall face
has a position error equal to its residual spread divided by the square root
of an effective sample size, where the effective count is the face length
divided by a 0.25 m correlation length, never the raw point count, because
points 2 cm apart on one wall are not independent measurements. Systematic: a
depth scale bias, default 1%, scaling with the measured length, plus a 1 cm
absolute floor below which nothing is claimed. Half width is
`1.96 * sqrt(sigma_a^2 + sigma_b^2 + (bias * L)^2)`, floored at 1 cm.

Observed on the supplied scans under the shipped default: the median interval
is 2.4% of the wall length, and the longest wall found, 4.186 m, is reported
with plus or minus 8.3 cm
(`fix_loop/before/bench/runs/c7d28f72c6/plan.json`, rebuilt by
`fix_loop/regenerate.sh before`).

**Ceiling height.** Measured per 25 cm grid cell as the ceiling mean minus the
floor plane beneath it, reported as the median. The half width combines the
cell-to-cell spread with the depth scale term. **The spread is deliberately
not divided by the square root of the cell count**: cells are not independent
samples of one number, and claiming sub-millimetre precision from a handheld
scan is exactly the confident-garbage failure the grading penalises. Where the
ceiling covers under 15% of floor cells, no number is invented: a 2.2 to 3.2 m
prior is emitted with method `prior_no_ceiling_observed` and a warning. Two of
the three supplied scans hit that path. The third measured 3.06 to 3.08 m
across rooms, consistent to 1.5 cm.

**Areas** propagate from the edge lengths, relative errors combined in
quadrature over the two axes, which is exact for a rectangle.

**Partially observed rooms** have their length and area intervals doubled, and
say so in the method string and in a warning. A room whose outline is under
80% backed by observed wall is a lower bound, not a measurement. Threshold
chosen from synthetic scenes: closed rooms measure 0.94 to 1.00 perimeter
support, a room with one wall missing measures 0.68.

**Damage extent** with depth is an interval from a colour-contrast estimate to
the detection box projected on the surface, because the box bounds the mark
and is not the mark. Without depth the region carries method
`unbounded_no_metric_depth`, an interval of 0 to 4 m2 and a warning.

### Ground truth, and its limits

Stated plainly, because the error budget is only as good as what it is checked
against:

* **The supplied LiDAR scans have no ground truth at all.** Nobody measured
  that property. No accuracy number for the LiDAR tier exists or is given.
* **Own rooms: tape, centimetres to the nearest inch.** `truth_uncertainty_m`
  is 0.013 and the coverage check widens the truth by it before asking whether
  an interval covers it, so a pipeline is not marked wrong for being more
  precise than the reference.
* **The hall is scored on ceiling height, openings and adjacency only**
  (`score_walls: false`). It is an open-plan connector taking in the corridor
  and laundry, with no tape-measurable wall run.
* **Windows were not measured.** Four are marked `present_unmeasured`: a
  prediction there is neither a hit nor a phantom, and contributes no
  calibration item.
* **Staged damage is two A4 sheets**, 21.0 x 29.7 cm, recorded as
  `extent_m2_upper_bound: 0.0624`, because a sheet bounds the mark it stands
  for rather than being it.

## 5. Calibration analysis

Calibration is scored separately from accuracy, in `cozmo/eval/calibration.py`:
empirical coverage against the nominal 95%, mean interval width as a
percentage of value, and a "confident garbage" count, meaning the truth falls
outside the interval and that interval is narrower than the median for its
quantity type. The median is per quantity because widths in metres and in
square metres are not comparable. Broken down by tier and by quantity.

**Empirical coverage for any tier is PENDING.** It waits on predictions for
the hand-measured rooms, which waits on the photo and video tiers. The
supplied LiDAR scans cannot contribute: no ground truth means no coverage to
compute.

What can be said now is the shape of the intervals the LiDAR tier produces,
and where honesty is enforced structurally rather than statistically. Two of
three supplied scans report ceiling height as a prior rather than a
measurement. Every room in the largest scan is flagged partially observed with
doubled intervals. Both are cases where the pipeline declines to produce a
tight number it cannot support, which is the behaviour the calibration score
is meant to reward, measured here by inspection rather than by coverage.

## 6. The fix loop

One loop was run on cross-capture repeatability. **It is a negative result and
is reported as one** (`fix_loop/`).

**The declaration.** Worst gate: repeatability, 0 of 112 wall rows within
tolerance on the two captures of one apartment. Root cause hypothesis: room
partition is unstable because it is grown from observed floor and the walked
path, so it is a function of where the operator walked. Predicted after the
fix: 40% of rows within tolerance, range 30 to 55%.

**The eval was broken first.** Plans are emitted in the ARKit world frame,
whose orientation depends on where the capture started, so the two captures
sat 58 degrees apart and no quarter turn aligned them. Every wall pair failed
the parallel test, which looked like total failure but was an impossible
comparison. Fixed by canonicalising each plan to its own Manhattan frame
before the quarter-turn search, in commits before the BEFORE run was taken.

**The falsifier fired.** The declaration named a cheap test: if dropping ghost
faces alone fixed room counts, the cell complex was unnecessary. It did not.
Ghost rejection removes about 1% of face length and the gate did not move
(`fix_loop/experiments/ghost_only/RESULT.md`). That also showed the
declaration's own evidence had over-counted ghosts at 16%, because it measured
faces outside an envelope built from the polygons under test.

**The fix shipped and did not work.** Wall-driven cell complex segmentation,
support taken from the 1.0 to 1.6 m band (chosen because the higher band the
plots suggested does not exist in one capture: only 10.0% of its wall points
lie between 1.6 and 2.2 m). Result: still 0 rows within tolerance, rooms
paired across the captures fell from 6 to 3, footprint areas inflated. Three
of four predictions were wrong. `erosion` is the default again and `cells`
stays selectable.

**The real cause was the third falsifier: coverage.** Only **56% and 37%** of
each capture's wall-face length has any counterpart in the other. The two
captures did not look at the same things.

**The contrast that carries the finding**, from
`fix_loop/evidence/evidence.json`, rebuilt by `fix_loop/evidence_run.py`:

| measured at | disagreement between the two captures |
|---|---|
| wall face position | **1.9 cm** median |
| polygon edge length | **97.5 cm** median |

The geometry is sound to two centimetres. The partition into rooms and edges
is not, and no segmentation rule can invent a wall one capture never saw. The
sample pair is now labelled `coverage_mismatched` and is not presented as a
valid repeat. The official repeat pair is `bedroom_2` against
`bedroom_2_repeat`: same-device at the video tier and the primary evidence,
cross-device at the photo tier and labelled as mixing camera differences into
any disagreement. Both PENDING on their tiers.

## 7. Known failure modes

**Measured in the repository.**

* **Cross-capture repeatability fails completely.** 0 of 153 rows. One loop
  did not fix it. It needs a capture-protocol or gate-denominator change.
* **Damage precision on photos.** 9 false regions in a room with 2 marks, from
  63 before filtering. On a LiDAR capture the same filters reach 0 from 106.
  The gap is structural: two of the four filters need depth and poses
  (`scripts/damage_ablation.py`, `docs/damage_eval/README.md`).
* **Cracks are the class most at risk.** The crop verifier that removes the
  false positives also removes the crack at threshold 0.20. A thin dark line
  on paint reads as a clean wall to SigLIP. The shipped threshold is 0.15 for
  this reason.
* **Room segmentation over-segments.** 11 rooms on a flat with perhaps 6 or 7
  spaces. Two captures of one property give different counts.
* **Ceiling height is often unavailable.** Two of three supplied scans never
  observed enough ceiling, so they report a 1 m wide prior.

**Hazards seen in the real captures.**

* **Glass shower screen** and a **wardrobe mirror**: both return confident
  depth for a room that is not there. Handled for damage by the geometry and
  multi-view filters, which take that scan from 106 false regions to 0. Not
  handled in reconstruction: mirrored geometry still contributes wall faces.
* **A dog moved through the capture.** Moving objects leave smeared points
  that no static-scene assumption covers.
* **Glossy floor tiles** return depth from the reflection as well as the
  surface, which thickens the floor peak.
* **Textureless white walls** give sparse returns near the LiDAR range limit,
  which is part of why coverage differs so much between two walks of one flat.
* **A ceiling never observed** is the commonest single cause of a wide
  interval, and it is entirely a capture-protocol problem: one deliberate tilt
  upward per room fixes it.

**Assumptions that will eventually break.**

* **Manhattan**: walls meet at right angles after one global rotation. A
  curved or 45-degree wall appears as a missing face rather than a wrong one,
  which is the safer failure, but it is still a failure. Recorded in every
  plan's `assumptions`.
* **Video-tier scale depends on the camera being held at about 1.4 m.** The
  capture protocol says so twice. A clip shot at waist height will be wrong by
  the ratio of the heights, and nothing in the pipeline can detect it.
