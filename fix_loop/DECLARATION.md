## The worst gate and its failing number

**repeatability, 0 of 112 wall rows within tolerance (0%), worst ratio infinite.**

Pair: `1a8384c3f6` and `c7d28f72c6`, two LiDAR captures of one apartment.
Tolerance is the larger of 1 cm and 0.5% of wall length.

| quantity | before |
|---|---|
| wall rows scored | 112 |
| rows within tolerance | 0 |
| rows failing because no counterpart exists | 92 |
| matched wall pairs | 20 |
| matched pairs within tolerance | 0 |
| worst matched pair | 2.371 m against 0.207 m |
| rooms found | 8 and 10 |
| rooms paired at IoU 0.3 or more | 6 |

Every other gate either passes or is not scored on these captures. The
captures have no ground truth, so no accuracy gate applies to them;
repeatability is the only gate that can fail here, and it fails completely.

Part of the earlier "0 of 34" was an eval artefact, as hypothesis H2
suspected. Plans are emitted in the ARKit world frame, so these two sit 58
degrees apart and no quarter turn aligned them; every wall pair failed the
parallel test. That is fixed in commits `b3b09b9` and `4780b2e`, before the
BEFORE run was taken. The number above is what remains after the eval was
corrected, so it is a real failure, not a comparison artefact.

## Root cause

**H1, with a precise mechanism. The wall geometry is repeatable; the partition
of that geometry into rooms is not.**

Rooms are currently grown from observed floor plus the walked path, eroded by
a fixed 0.5 m. That makes the room partition a function of where the operator
walked and looked, which differs between captures, rather than a function of
the building, which does not.

## Evidence

All in `fix_loop/evidence/`, regenerated by `fix_loop/evidence_run.py`.

**a. Where both captures saw a wall, they agree to 2 cm.** After registration
(footprint IoU 0.68), the median perpendicular offset between matched wall
faces is **1.9 cm**, inside the 5 cm repeatability tolerance. See
`a_wall_faces_overlay.png`: the two sets of faces trace the same building.

**a2. The same walls produce polygon edges that differ by a metre.** Median
absolute difference of matched wall-pair lengths is **0.975 m**, against that
1.9 cm face offset. The two captures cut the same physical wall into different
staircase edges, so the gate compares a 2.37 m wall against a 0.21 m fragment.
Geometry agrees; the decomposition into rooms and edges does not.

**b. Room count is controlled by the erosion radius, and the two captures
respond differently.** `b_erosion_sensitivity.png`:

| erosion radius m | 0.3 | 0.4 | 0.5 | 0.6 | 0.7 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| rooms, 1a8384c3f6 | 7 | 8 | 9 | 7 | 6 |
| rooms, c7d28f72c6 | 16 | 16 | 11 | 6 | 6 |

At the shipped 0.5 m the two captures disagree by two rooms. Total area for
`c00a170fe1` jumps from 13.7 m2 to 24.6 m2 between 0.4 and 0.5. A fixed
radius is the wrong control: it is a morphological accident, not a property of
the building.

**c. Free space is fragmented and leaks.** Connected components of free space
before erosion: 4, 24 and 60 for the three scans. Free area reaches 102% and
114% of the room envelope for the two apartment scans, so free space spills
outside the building it is supposed to be inside.

**d. Ghost faces are a real but secondary contributor.** 16.4% and 16.1% of
structural wall-face length lies outside the room envelope (15 and 29
segments). See `d_ghost_faces.png`. This supports H3, but it cannot account
for a total failure on its own.

**e. The usable height band is lower than assumed.** Median wall-point height
is 0.87 m, 0.97 m and 1.42 m. Only 2.7% and 10.0% of wall points in
`c00a170fe1` and `1a8384c3f6` lie between 1.6 and 2.2 m. The highest band well
covered in both apartment captures is roughly **1.0 to 1.6 m**, not 1.0 to
2.2 m. `1a8384c3f6` mostly looks down, as suspected.

## Intended fix

Wall-driven segmentation, as proposed, with one change forced by evidence e.

1. Extend structural wall faces into an axis-aligned cell complex in the
   Manhattan frame.
2. Mark cells interior using the camera path and floor observations.
3. Cut between adjacent cells only where the shared edge has wall support.
   Support is measured in the **1.0 to 1.6 m** band, the highest band well
   covered in both apartment captures, rather than 1.0 to 2.2 m, which
   `1a8384c3f6` barely reaches.
4. A gap of up to about 1.6 m in an otherwise supported edge is an opening,
   not a reason to merge.
5. Rooms are the connected components of the cut complex.
6. Rooms with unobserved sides are flagged partially observed and carry
   widened intervals, instead of being closed by guesswork.
7. Ghost faces are dropped by a support and visibility test, which should also
   remove most of the 16% of face length lying outside the envelope.

The erosion radius disappears as a parameter. Room boundaries then come from
wall support, which both captures measure to 2 cm, instead of from observed
floor coverage, which they do not share.

## Predicted after number

| quantity | before | predicted after |
|---|---|---|
| repeatability rows within tolerance | 0 of 112 (0%) | **40%**, range 30% to 55% |
| median length difference, matched pairs | 0.975 m | under 0.15 m |
| rooms found, the two apartment captures | 8 and 10 | 6 to 7 each, differing by at most 1 |
| structural face length outside the envelope | 16.1% and 16.4% | under 8% |

The ceiling on the first row is set by coverage, not by the fix: only 56% and
37% of each capture's wall-face length has any counterpart in the other, since
the two captures did not look at the same things. Rows with no counterpart
count as failures by design, so a perfect partition still cannot reach 100%.
That is why the prediction is 40% and not 90%.

## What would prove this wrong

* **Wall geometry, not segmentation, is the problem** if after the fix the
  matched wall pairs still differ by more than 0.15 m at the median. Evidence
  a already argues against this, since matched faces sit 1.9 cm apart, but a
  post-fix median above 0.15 m would move the cause upstream to pose drift or
  face extraction.
* **The height band choice is wrong** if wall support taken from 1.0 to 1.6 m
  produces fewer cuts than the current method in `1a8384c3f6`, that is if room
  count there falls below 5.
* **Coverage, not method, dominates** if room counts converge to 6 or 7 but
  the within-tolerance share stays below 15%. That would mean the unmatched
  rows are the whole story and the gate is measuring capture overlap rather
  than reconstruction repeatability, which would be an argument to change the
  gate's denominator rather than the pipeline.
* **Ghosts are the real cause** if dropping ghost faces alone, with the
  erosion segmentation left in place, brings room counts to within one of each
  other. That is a cheap experiment and worth running first.

# ADDENDUM, written after the fix was attempted

The original text above is unchanged. This records what happened to it.

**The falsifier fired negative, so the cell complex was built as declared.**
Dropping ghost faces alone left the gate at 0 rows within tolerance and moved
room counts apart by one. See `experiments/ghost_only/RESULT.md`.

**The fix shipped and did not work.** The repeatability gate still fails at 0
of 153 rows. Rooms paired across the two apartment captures fell from 6 to 3
and footprint areas inflated. Full numbers in `POSTMORTEM.md` and `DIFF.md`.

**Evidence d above should not be trusted.** It reported 16% of wall-face
length outside the room envelope. That envelope came from the room polygons
produced by the segmentation under test, which were themselves inflated, so
the measure over-counted ghosts. A visibility test that asks whether anyone
stood beside the face puts the real figure near 1%. Hypothesis H3 is not a
cause of anything here.

**The root cause named above was incomplete.** Room partition is unstable,
which is true, but the deeper cause is that the two captures observe
different subsets of the building: only 56% and 37% of each capture's
wall-face length has any counterpart in the other. No segmentation rule can
invent a wall one capture never saw. The third falsifier listed above,
"coverage, not method, dominates", is the one that fired.

**One number from the declaration held up.** Matched wall faces agree to
1.9 cm. The geometry is sound; the partition and the capture coverage are
not.
